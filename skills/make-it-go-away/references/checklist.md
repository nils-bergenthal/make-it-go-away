# The things people forget

This list runs in the background during stage 3. It is not a questionnaire.

- Go through the points silently. Many are answered by the case.
- Ask about a point only if the case touches it and the answer changes what gets built.
- Put every other gap under "still open", with who might know. A point you did not ask about is "still open", never "there is none".
- The questions below are examples, not scripts. Ask in the user's language.

## 1. People affected
- "Who gets something different from you once this runs?"
- "Who would be annoyed if nobody told them?"

## 2. Trigger
- "What tells you today that it is time to do this?"
- "What happens if two cases arrive at the same moment?"

## 3. Data sources
- "Where do you look things up while you do this?"
- "Is anything only in a person's head?"

## 4. Errors and who gets told
Turn each answer into a test case with a concrete input.
- "If a case fails, who should get a message?"
- "How would you notice if it stopped running a week ago?"
- "If it runs twice by accident, what must not happen twice?"

## 5. Rights and accounts
- "Whose account would this run under?"
- "What happens when that person leaves or changes the password?"

Never ask for the password itself.

## 6. Data protection
- "Does a case contain details about customers, staff or patients?"
- "Does that data leave your company on the way?"

Rules differ by country. Do not quote laws or articles. Say "the data protection rules that apply to you". For sensitive data (health, children, money) remind the user to check with the responsible person. If the user names a rule themselves, such as GDPR or HIPAA, take over their word. It is a reminder, not legal advice.

## 7. Who has to agree
- "Do your computer people (IT) have to agree to a new tool?"
- "Does the person for data protection have to look at it?"
- "Does your boss have to sign off the cost?"

In Germany and similar countries also: "Is there a works council (Betriebsrat), and does it have to agree?" It matters when the tool can show what individual staff do. Elsewhere ask: "Does a body that represents the staff have to agree?"

## 8. Cost and limits
- "Is there a monthly amount it must stay under?"

One sentence on cost is enough. No profit calculation.

## 9. Back to the old way
- "If it breaks on Monday, can you do it by hand again?"
- "Can a wrong result be taken back?"

## 10. Measuring success
- "What would make you say after a month: this was worth it?"

## 11. Test with real cases before the start
- "Do you have ten old cases we can try it on first?"
- "Who checks the first results by hand, and for how long?"
