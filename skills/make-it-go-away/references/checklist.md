# The things people forget

This list runs in the background during stage 3. It is not a questionnaire.

How to use it:

- Go through the points silently. Many are already answered by the case.
- Ask about a point if the case touches it and the answer changes what gets built.
- Put every other gap under "still open". Add who might know.
- A point you did not ask about is "still open". Never write "there is none" unless the user said so.
- Ask in the user's language. The questions below are examples, not scripts.
- The sources are names for looking things up. They are reminders, not legal advice.

## 1. People affected

Who notices when this changes, and who has to agree?

- "Who gets something different from you once this runs?"
- "Who would be annoyed if nobody told them?"
- "Who has to agree before it starts?"

## 2. Trigger

What starts the task, and how does anyone know it is time?

- "What tells you today that it is time to do this?"
- "Does it start on a set day, or when something arrives?"
- "What happens if two cases arrive at the same moment?"

## 3. Data sources

Where does the information come from, and can it be trusted?

- "Where do you look things up while you do this?"
- "Which of these places is sometimes wrong or out of date?"
- "Is anything only in a person's head?"

## 4. Errors and who gets told

What happens when it goes wrong, and who hears about it? Turn each answer into a test case with a concrete input.

- "If a case fails, who should get a message?"
- "Should it stop, or carry on with the next case?"
- "How would you notice if it stopped running a week ago?"
- "If it runs twice by accident, what must not happen twice?"

## 5. Rights and accounts

Whose account does it use, and what may it do?

- "Whose account would this run under?"
- "What happens when that person leaves or changes the password?"
- "What should it be allowed to read, and what to change?"

Never ask for the password itself.

## 6. Data protection

Does it touch details about people?

- "Does a case contain details about customers, staff or patients?"
- "Does that data leave your company on the way?"
- "Is there someone for data protection you can ask?"

Rules on data protection differ by country. Do not quote laws or articles. Say "the data protection rules that apply to you". For sensitive data (health, children, money) remind the user to check with the responsible person. If the user names a rule themselves, such as GDPR or HIPAA, take over their word. It is a reminder to check, not legal advice.

## 7. Approvals

Who has to agree before it starts?

- "Do your computer people (IT) have to agree to a new tool?"
- "Does the person for data protection have to look at it?"
- "Does your boss have to sign off the cost?"

**Germany and similar countries:**

- "Is there a works council (Betriebsrat), and does it have to agree?"

This matters when the tool can show what individual staff do.

**All other countries, neutral question:**

- "Does a body that represents the staff have to agree?"

## 8. Cost and limits

What may it cost, and where does it stop?

- "Is there a monthly amount it must stay under?"
- "Is there a number of cases per day it should not exceed?"
- "Who pays for the tool or the account?"

No profit calculation. One sentence on cost is enough.

## 9. Back to the old way

Can the work go on without it?

- "If it breaks on Monday, can you do it by hand again?"
- "Who still knows the old way in a year?"
- "Can a wrong result be taken back?"

## 10. Measuring success

How will anyone know it worked?

- "What would make you say after a month: this was worth it?"
- "Which number should go down, or which one up?"

## 11. Test with real cases before the start

Has it seen real cases before it runs for real?

- "Do you have ten old cases we can try it on first?"
- "Who checks the first results by hand?"
- "For how long should a person look over its shoulder?"
